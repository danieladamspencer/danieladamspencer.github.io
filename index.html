About

I'm a scientist at Verisk, where I build and validate statistical models of tropical cyclones and lend a hand with the risk from other atmospheric perils, like winter storms and severe thunderstorms.

Before that I was a research associate at Indiana University, developing Bayesian methods and open-source R software for analyzing fMRI data on the cortical surface. I earned my PhD in Statistical Science at UC Santa Cruz, where my dissertation on Bayesian tensor regression was a finalist for ISBA's Savage Award. Long before any of that, I taught high-school math in Portuguese as a Peace Corps volunteer in Zóbuè, Mozambique.
